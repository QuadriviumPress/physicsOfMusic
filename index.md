title: Physics of Music
subtitle: Vibration, Hearing, Instruments, and the Sound of a Room
---

*Physics of Music* is an open textbook about how musical sound is made, how it
travels, and how it is heard. It begins with a vibrating object and ends with a
recording of one, and along the way it asks the questions a musician asks and
answers them with physics: why a string sounds a pitch at all, why a clarinet
and a trumpet playing the same note are unmistakably different, why some pairs
of notes sound settled and others restless, why a piano cannot be tuned
perfectly, and why the same quartet sounds glorious in one hall and muddy in
the next.

No calculus is required. The main argument runs on algebra, trigonometry, and
ratios; the derivations that genuinely need calculus are set aside in dropdowns
that can be opened or ignored. No musical training is assumed either — the
notation the book uses is introduced where it is first needed. See the
[Preface](front/preface.md) for the audience, the organization, and how the
printed editions differ.

Where the text describes a sound, there is usually a figure showing what is in
it and a link to hear it; where a moving picture teaches more than a static one,
the web edition has a running simulation. Every sound example is synthesized
rather than recorded, so that a comparison can hold everything fixed but the one
thing it is about.
