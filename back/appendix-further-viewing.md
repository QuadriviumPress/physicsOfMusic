Every claim in this book can be read; several of them are more convincing
watched. The videos below are not part of the book — nothing here is adapted
from them, quoted, or required for any exercise — they are pointers to the
same physics, demonstrated by people who film it well. Several are from
general-audience science channels (Veritasium, Smarter Every Day, Steve
Mould, and their kin); others are lectures, research footage, or a
professionally produced music video. Inclusion here does not depend on
licence: these are ordinary citations to outside work, the same as a journal
reference in `back/references.bib`, not content incorporated into the book
(see [`SOURCES.md`](https://github.com/QuadriviumPress/physicsOfMusic/blob/main/SOURCES.md)
for that distinction).

Links were checked in September 2026. YouTube channels occasionally rename,
re-upload, or take a video down; if a link is dead, search the title and
creator given, most have mirrors or are still posted under the same name.

## Chapter 1 — Sound, Music, and Simple Harmonic Motion

**[Pendulum Waves](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kPhTNwOEngk)** — Harvard
Natural Sciences Lecture Demonstrations. Fifteen simple pendulums of
carefully graded lengths, released together, drift in and out of phase and
produce traveling waves, standing waves, and apparent randomness — all from
nothing but fifteen independent copies of the simple harmonic motion this
chapter opens with.

**[Why Sound Can't Travel in a Vacuum](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YxUERaXBAU8)**
— the Royal Institution. A ringing bell fades as the air is pumped from its
jar even though the mechanism continues to move, a two-minute demonstration
that sound needs a material medium.

## Chapter 2 — Wave Motion and the Speed of Sound

**[Traveling Waves, Sound Waves, and Energy in Waves](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Tul2JfUEnpA)**
— Walter Lewin, MIT 8.03 *Vibrations and Waves*. A lecture-demonstration
treatment of how a disturbance propagates and carries energy without carrying
matter, the idea this chapter turns into $v = f\lambda$.

## Chapter 3 — Superposition, Interference, and Standing Waves

**[Traveling Waves and Standing Waves](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hZCsnd257w0)**
— Walter Lewin, MIT 8.02. Live rope and string demonstrations of reflection,
superposition, and the standing-wave patterns that follow from fixed
boundaries — the mechanism behind every fretted or open string in the book.

**[Standing Wave Harmonics](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PVX4V5Adbzk)**
— xmdemo. A compact laboratory demonstration in which successive resonances
make the nodes, antinodes, and first several string modes directly visible.

## Chapter 4 — Resonance and Normal Modes

**[The Tacoma Narrows Fallacy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6ai2QFxStxo)**
— MinutePhysics. The bridge collapse is the textbook picture of resonance in
almost every other textbook; this one explains why that picture is wrong
(aeroelastic flutter, not a driven resonance) — useful precisely because it
sharpens what "resonance" does and does not mean.

**[Galloping Gertie: Resonance and the Tacoma Narrows Bridge](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l5Kt0xs_nTk)**
— the original 1940 collapse footage, for the demonstration itself before
watching the correction above.

**[Tuning Forks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aCocQa2Bcuc)** — MIT Physics
Instructional Resources Lab. A short demonstration of natural frequency and
sympathetic vibration: one resonator responds when another drives it at the
frequency it already prefers.

## Chapter 5 — Fourier Analysis, Harmonics, and Timbre

**[But What Is the Fourier Transform? A Visual Introduction](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=spUNpyF58BY)**
— 3Blue1Brown. The winding-around-a-circle picture of the transform that
turns a waveform into a spectrum, built up visually rather than from the
integral this chapter states.

## Chapter 6 — The Ear and the Physiology of Hearing

**[The Science of Hearing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LkGOGzpbrCk)** —
TED-Ed, with Douglas L. Oliver. Follows a sound from the outer ear through
the ossicles to the cochlea, the anatomical tour this chapter gives in prose.

**[Journey of Sound to the Brain](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eQEaiZ2j9oc)**
— National Institutes of Health. A concise anatomical animation following
sound through the eardrum and ossicles, into the cochlea and hair cells, and
finally along the auditory nerve.

**[These Illusions Fool Almost Everyone](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Sn07AMCfaAI)**
— Veritasium. Audio illusions that only work because of *how* the ear and
brain localize and interpret sound, not because of anything unusual in the
sound itself.

## Chapter 7 — Loudness, Decibels, and the Equal-Loudness Contours

**[Decibel Scale](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_p-WyPg1sbU)** — Khan
Academy. A short, careful walkthrough of why loudness is measured on a
logarithmic scale and what a 10 dB step actually means, the groundwork this
chapter builds the equal-loudness contours on.

## Chapter 8 — Pitch, Beats, Consonance, and Dissonance

**[Music and Measure Theory](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cyW5z-M2yzw)** —
3Blue1Brown. Connects a classical question about rational numbers to why
some intervals sound settled and others restless — the same territory as
this chapter's beats and roughness arguments, from a different direction.

**[Unison Tuning](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mzoBH-HbKmw)** — Piano
Technicians Guild. A working piano tuner demonstrates the audible beats in
an imperfect unison and the clean sound obtained as the strings converge on
the same frequency.

## Chapter 9 — Musical Scales and Tuning Systems

**[Equal Temperament vs. Just Intonation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gtkeDDcXu9M)**
— Adam Neely. A working musician's case for what is gained and lost in the
compromise this chapter derives from the syntonic and Pythagorean commas.

## Chapter 10 — String Instruments

**[Bowed Violin String in Slow Motion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6JeyiM0YNo4)**
— high-speed footage of Helmholtz motion, the stick-slip sawtooth corner
traveling back and forth along a bowed string that this chapter describes
but cannot show moving.

## Chapter 11 — Wind Instruments and Air-Column Resonance

**[The Physics of Brass Instruments, Pt. 1: The Lips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tGdyrXz4Wg8)**
— a physicist's explanation of the lips as the oscillating valve in a brass
instrument, and the air column as the resonator that selects its pitch — the
feedback loop [Chapter 4](#ch-resonance) flags as unique to winds.

## Chapter 12 — Percussion: Membranes, Bars, and Plates

**[Singing Plates: Standing Waves on Chladni Plates](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wYoxOJDrZzw)**
— Physics Girl. Sand on a vibrating plate collects along the nodal lines of
its two-dimensional modes, made visible directly, the inharmonic mode
pattern this chapter contrasts with a string's.

**[Cymatics: Science vs. Music](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q3oItpVa9fs)**
— Nigel Stanford. A produced music video built entirely from real cymatic
and resonance demonstrations (Chladni plate, Ruben's tube, Tesla coil,
ferrofluid speaker) synced to the soundtrack driving them.

**[Seeing Sound With Sand](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-CZlrgq8syE)** —
BBC Earth Science. A slower explanatory treatment of Chladni patterns: sand
migrates away from the moving regions of a resonant plate and collects along
its nodal lines.

## Chapter 13 — The Singing Voice

**[High-Speed Video of the Vocal Folds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9kHdhbEnhoA)**
— laryngeal high-speed imaging of the vocal folds oscillating during
phonation, the vibrator this chapter treats as the voice's counterpart to a
reed or a string.

**[Singing in the MRI: Making the Voice Visible](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J3TwTb-T044)**
— Tyley Ross. Real-time MRI reveals the changing tongue, jaw, lips, and vocal
tract while a singer changes vowels and pitch — the filter half of the
source--filter model in motion.

## Chapter 14 — Room Acoustics and Concert-Hall Design

**[Boston Symphony Hall Is "Acoustically Perfect": Secrets Behind the Scenes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AXmBgeRHbiY)**
— a tour of the hall Wallace Sabine designed using the reverberation-time
formula this chapter derives, still cited as one of the finest-sounding halls
in the world.

**[Saxophone in Reverberation Room and Anechoic Chamber](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6ssGj65U2F8)**
— Hello.Henning. The same instrument in two acoustic extremes, compressed
into forty seconds: first surrounded by a long reverberant tail, then almost
entirely stripped of reflected sound.

## Chapter 15 — Electronic and Recorded Sound

**[How Do Vinyl Records Hold Stereo Sound?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3DdUvoc7tJ4)**
— Technology Connections. The 45/45 groove geometry that encodes two
channels in one groove wall, explained at the same mechanical level as this
chapter's account of the medium.

**[Vinyl LP in Electron Microscope](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GuCdsyCWmt8)**
— Ben Krasnow, *Applied Science*. A record groove imaged directly, the
physical waveform a stylus traces that this chapter's account of analog
recording otherwise leaves abstract.

**[How Do Speakers Work?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jhg90zsjqt4)** —
Branch Education. A cutaway animation follows the electrical signal through
the magnet and voice coil to the moving cone and the pressure wave radiated
into the room.
